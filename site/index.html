Back on track?

Australia’s uneven rail recovery

Rail travel has recovered unevenly. Compare Australian cities, then look closer at Victoria to see how where and when people travel shape the picture.

The national picture

The scale of today’s network is only part of the story. The following comparisons use the Yearbook’s city estimates and their own 2018–2019 baseline.

Keep the denominator in view

Quiet stations can double without offsetting losses at busy ones. The area map uses summed entries, not average station percentages.

Station entries also differ from train patronage: they exclude transfers within a station. Southern Cross entries exclude the gates to regional platforms 1–8.

The daily pattern has shifted

Recovery also depends on when people travel. Start with weekday time bands, then compare station recovery and the changing balance of weekend travel.

Back on track —
but not back to the same pattern.

Perth has returned to its pre-pandemic city estimate; Sydney and Melbourne remain below. Victoria’s regional trains take a larger share, while Melbourne’s station entries remain concentrated in the centre. Weekend travel has strengthened relative to weekdays.

Sources & methods

Keyboard: focus a chart, then use Left and Right arrow keys to inspect values.

Data sources

Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics: Trainline network totals and separate Yearbook city estimates.

Department of Transport and Planning: station entries and monthly train patronage. Station estimates are rounded to 50.

Australian Bureau of Statistics: boundaries and city locations.

Comparisons

Financial years run July–June. Station recovery compares 2018–2019 with 2024–2025 using the same 220 stations.

Area recovery = total current entries ÷ total baseline entries × 100. Areas describe station locations, not residents’ travel. Equal-area grids retain all 222 current stations.

National figures use report tables; historical Trainline workbook checks remain incomplete.

Methods and sensitivity checks

National comparisons

Different series: Yearbook city estimates include Sydney intercity, a Melbourne regional-commuter allowance and Brisbane Airtrain. These values are not the Trainline totals above. Years on the axis end in June. Colour and dash pattern identify cities; the selector highlights one while retaining the others for context.

Read top to bottom: begin with the 2018–2019 total, then add each city’s gain or loss. Floating bars show the contribution to the running total. This is arithmetic, not a causal explanation.

Victorian train journeys

Each square is about 1% of metropolitan plus V/Line train journeys. Shares are rounded to whole squares. Regional shares: 8.0% in 2018–2019 and 11.8% in 2024–2025, shown together for comparison. Coach, bus and tram categories are excluded.

Both start at 100. Actual totals: metropolitan 243.19 million → 187.40 million; V/Line 21.04 million → 25.06 million. Two endpoints hide intervening changes. Metro estimation began using available barrier counts in January 2021; V/Line figures include replacement services.

Station maps

An area’s current entries ÷ baseline entries × 100, using the same stations in both sums. Dots locate stations. Grey areas have no eligible matched stations. This measures stations located in an area, not travel by its residents. Australian Bureau of Statistics local government boundaries (2025); Melbourne-centred azimuthal equal-area projection.

All 222 current stations; totals are conserved for every grid. Whole square cells in Australian equal-area coordinates (reference number 3577), displayed with the same Melbourne-centred azimuthal equal-area projection as the adjacent map. The fixed square-root colour scale makes smaller totals more visible; equal colour steps are not equal increments in entries. Cells are not clipped at the coast. The largest cell’s share changes from 20.4% to 43.0% across 2.5–10 kilometres grids.

Station comparisons

Signed change in annual entries, 2018–2019 to 2024–2025. The remaining 210 matched stations are omitted from this selected view. Stable station identifiers are matched; differences in data presence are not treated as station openings or closures.

2024–2025 publisher-defined normal-weekday bands have unequal durations: colour is a share of the five-band sum, not an hourly rate. Twelve largest annual-entry stations among 66 eligible; exclude any band at the 50-entry floor, normal weekday below 1,000, or band-total discrepancy over 150.

Weekend distribution

Ratio = average of Saturday and Sunday daily entries ÷ average weekday entries. Box: middle half of stations; white line: median; whiskers: observations within 1.5 box-lengths; dots: outliers. Each station has equal weight. Both years require weekday ≥500 and weekend values above the 50-entry floor.

Station recovery sensitivity

Of 220 matched identifiers, 219 pass the default 5,000-entry baseline screen; the weekend boxplot uses 191 stations with eligible values in both years. Each station counts once in this histogram; bars group recovery into intervals 20 points wide. The dashed line marks a return to baseline. All eligible extremes remain visible; 100 means recovery, not 100% growth.
