Australia’s uneven rail recovery

More journeys do not necessarily mean a return to the old pattern. A national comparison and a closer look at Melbourne reveal a recovery that depends on where — and how — you travel.

The national picture

To compare recovery, hold the statistical definition steady. The two charts below use the Yearbook’s city estimates throughout, including their own pre-pandemic baseline.

Journeys, not people. Someone travelling to work and home may appear twice. Counts do not tell us why people travelled, where they live, or whether working from home caused a change.

Different rail journeys

Australia’s trains serve city trips, regional commuters and longer-distance travel. Separating those tasks changes the recovery story.

A newer Victorian update. The full Department of Transport and Planning file extends to June 2026. In 2025–2026 it records 202.31 million metropolitan and 27.10 million V/Line train journeys. Free-travel periods from November 2025 required alternative estimation, so those newer values are shown separately.

Where recovery lands

Melbourne / Victoria case study. Across 220 matched metropolitan station identifiers, annual entries are 77.1% of their 2018–2019 level. Relative change and current volume answer different questions.

Keep the denominator in view

A doubling at a quiet station cannot offset a modest fall at a busy one. The area map uses the ratio of summed entries, not the average of station percentages.

Station entries also differ from train patronage: they exclude transfers within a station. Southern Cross entries exclude the gates to regional platforms 1–8.

The daily pattern has shifted

Annual totals conceal when stations are used. Time bands and day-type ratios help describe the change without claiming a cause.

Back on track —
but not back to the same pattern.

The evidence supports uneven recovery: across cities, between metropolitan and regional services, and among Melbourne’s stations. Weekend use has become stronger relative to weekdays in the matched station cohort.

Sources & methods

Data sources

Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics: Trainline network totals and separate Yearbook city estimates.

Department of Transport and Planning: station entries and monthly train patronage. Station estimates are rounded to 50.

Australian Bureau of Statistics: boundaries and city locations.

Comparisons

Financial years run July–June. Station recovery compares 2018–2019 with 2024–2025 using the same 220 stations.

Area recovery = total current entries ÷ total baseline entries × 100. Areas describe station locations, not residents’ travel. Equal-area grids retain all 222 current stations.
