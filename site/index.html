Back on track?

Australia’s uneven rail recovery

Rail journeys are returning, but not equally. Compare cities and services, then explore how Melbourne's station use has changed.

Big cities, unfinished recovery

Different services, different recoveries

In Trainline’s table, non-urban rail includes intercity and regional services. Victoria’s regional trains tell a different recovery story from its metropolitan trains.

The recovery gap changes by place

Melbourne / Victoria case study. Station entries bring recovery down to place. Comparing the same 220 metropolitan stations in both years reveals local differences.

Rail use differs across stations and days

Station recovery varies by place; rail use varies across the week. Weekday rhythms differ between stations, while weekend use rose relative to weekdays.

Back on track —
but not back to the same pattern.

Perth has returned to its pre-pandemic level, but Sydney and Melbourne account for nearly all the five-city shortfall. In Victoria, metropolitan journeys fell 22.9% while V/Line journeys grew 19.1%. Weekend-to-weekday ratios increased at 176 of 191 eligible stations. Rail is returning, but not to the same pattern.

Sources & Methods

Data sources

Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics: Trainline provides urban and non-urban journey totals. Separate Yearbook estimates include Sydney intercity, Melbourne's regional-commuter allowance and Brisbane Airtrain. Their scopes differ.

Department of Transport and Planning: Station entries and monthly train patronage. Station estimates are rounded to 50 and exclude within-station transfers and Southern Cross regional-platform gates.

Metropolitan estimation changed in January 2021; V/Line includes replacement services. The analysis ends in 2024–2025, although monthly data extend into 2025–2026.

Australian Bureau of Statistics: Boundaries and city locations.

Comparisons

Financial years run July–June. Station recovery compares the same 220 stations across 2018–2019 and 2024–2025; 219 qualify at the histogram's default threshold.

Area recovery compares current and baseline station entries. Council areas represent station locations, not residents' travel. Equal-area grids include all 222 current stations.

Heatmap time bands have unequal durations; colours show shares, not hourly rates. Weekend ratios compare average daily entries, requiring at least 500 weekday entries.

Historical Trainline workbooks were unavailable, so cross-release totals remain unreconciled. Recovery charts instead use estimates from one Yearbook release.
