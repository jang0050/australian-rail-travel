Australia’s uneven rail recovery

More journeys do not necessarily mean a return to the old pattern. A national comparison and a closer look at Melbourne reveal a recovery that depends on where — and how — you travel.

A visual story in four parts · Common comparison: 2018–2019 to 2024–2025 · Latest Victorian update: 2025–2026

The national picture

To compare recovery, hold the statistical definition steady. The two charts below use the Yearbook’s city estimates throughout, including their own pre-pandemic baseline.

Journeys, not people. Someone travelling to work and home may appear twice. Counts do not tell us why people travelled, where they live, or whether working from home caused a change.

Different rail journeys

Australia’s trains serve city trips, regional commuters and longer-distance travel. Separating those tasks changes the recovery story.

A newer Victorian update. The full Department of Transport and Planning file extends to June 2026. In 2025–2026 it records 202.31 million metropolitan and 27.10 million V/Line train journeys. Free-travel periods from November 2025 required alternative estimation, so those newer values are shown separately.

Where recovery lands

Melbourne / Victoria case study. Across 220 matched metropolitan station identifiers, annual entries are 77.1% of their 2018–2019 level. Relative change and current volume answer different questions.

Keep the denominator in view

A doubling at a quiet station cannot offset a modest fall at a busy one. The area map uses the ratio of summed entries, not the average of station percentages.

Station entries also differ from train patronage: they exclude transfers within a station. Southern Cross entries exclude the gates to regional platforms 1–8.

The daily pattern has shifted

Annual totals conceal when stations are used. Time bands and day-type ratios help describe the change without claiming a cause.

Back on track —
but not back to the same pattern.

The evidence supports uneven recovery: across cities, between metropolitan and regional services, and among Melbourne’s stations. Weekend use has become stronger relative to weekdays in the matched station cohort.

These counts describe the pattern. They cannot, on their own, separate the effects of fares, service changes, population growth or changed work routines.

Sources & how to read the evidence

Three statistical objects

Trainline: reported operator-network journeys, 2024–2025. Sydney includes suburban Sydney Trains plus Metro; intercity is in the separate non-urban table.

Yearbook: city public-transport estimates. Sydney includes intercity; Melbourne includes an estimated regional-commuter allowance; Brisbane includes Airtrain. Never joined to Trainline values.

Department of Transport and Planning: metropolitan station entries and a separate monthly patronage dataset. Full releases are used; five-row data service previews are excluded. Counts are estimates, rounded to 50 at station level, and are less precise for individual stations than for the network.

National workbook downloads remain unavailable in this build. Complete published Trainline tables 15 and 23 and the Yearbook comparison values were checked against the official reports. The preferred Trainline history and cross-release reconciliation remain pending.

Reproducible comparisons

Financial years run July–June. All annual monthly totals require twelve unique months. Station records match on stable identifiers; 220 of 222 records occur in both endpoint releases. No station moved over 500 metres; all matched points joined unambiguously to the same local government area at both dates.

Area colour uses 100 × current sum ÷ baseline sum. Fixed recovery breaks distinguish below 60, 60–80, 80–100 and at least 100. The boundaries describe station locations, not catchments or resident travel.

The bins conserve all 178.48 million current station entries. Their Australian equal-area coordinate system (reference number 3577) origin is fixed at (0,0), with a separate half-cell-shift comparison. No passenger flows or individual locations are inferred.
